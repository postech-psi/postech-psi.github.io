The airframe is the physical connection between propulsion, flight electronics and recovery hardware. PSI’s public research includes carbon-fibre bodies and the dynamic stability of stage separation. The exterior photograph is a view of real hardware, not a cutaway of its internals.

From measurements to a flight-state estimate

The current Arduino Portenta H7 software separates sensor processing and flight decisions on M7 from SD logging and XBee telemetry on M4. Its UKF estimates vertical motion from acceleration and barometric altitude; GNSS supplies a separate position record. An integrated Python ground station supports live inspection and recorded-log playback.

Separate the flight loop
from the record keeping

The current Portenta H7 implementation divides responsibilities between M7 and M4. M7 reads the sensors, checks their health, updates vertical-motion estimates and evaluates flight state and recovery-actuation decisions. Its main loop is configured for 100 Hz.

A separate transmission thread forwards framed, checksum-checked inter-core messages containing measurements and status. M4 handles microSD storage and XBee telemetry; the radio path is configured for 25 Hz. Queues and drop counters make delays and lost records observable without placing radio or storage work inside the flight-decision loop.

These are configured software rates, not measured end-to-end performance guarantees. This describes the cited revision; a historical flight does not prove that all its current features flew on that mission.

A measurement is not
the same as an estimate

The IMU supplies attitude, acceleration and angular-rate data. The BMP390 provides barometric altitude relative to the pressure established at startup. The unscented Kalman filter (UKF) combines vertical acceleration and barometric altitude to estimate vertical motion. A fresh barometric sample triggers a measurement update, nominally about 50 Hz; an outer-loop tick alone does not make an old measurement new.

GNSS position and altitude are separate records for logging and recovery context. They are not inputs to this vertical-state UKF or to flight-state decisions in the cited revision. Barometric altitude, GNSS altitude, filtered altitude and predicted apogee have different meanings and references.

Sensor freshness and health

Sensor checks distinguish a responding device from a fresh usable measurement. A last-known value can remain visible when new samples stop arriving. Health flags, reference-pressure status, warnings and flight-state events help explain that condition. They are interpretation aids, not a flight-readiness certificate.

Onboard and
received logs

Each onboard session has a telemetry CSV and a companion text log. The numeric record carries measurements, estimates, state and health notes; the text record preserves initialization, calibration and state-change messages. M7 mission-time events and M4’s own messages use distinguishable clock labels.

The ground station records only what arrives over the radio. A radio interruption can remove samples from that record even when they were saved onboard. The onboard record is also subject to finite buffers, storage faults and interrupted writes. Drop counters and warnings support investigation; neither path is presented as lossless.

Ground-station displays
and playback

The integrated Python ground station combines 3D attitude, position history, flight statistics and timestamped telemetry/system messages. Selectable plots cover acceleration, angular rate, altitude, vertical velocity and predicted apogee; acceleration and altitude are visible by default. Barometric, GNSS and Kalman altitude traces can be compared, while position history uses a grid or a prepared offline map.

Compatible CSV and text logs can be reviewed without a live serial connection. Pause, seek and 1×, 2×, 5×, 10× or 50× playback rebuild the displayed history and statistics. IMU and barometer indicators distinguish fresh, stale and unavailable data. GNSS “ALIVE” describes the accepted data stream, not a guarantee of a valid position fix.

The statistics panel’s maximum is the largest received filtered-altitude value; predicted apogee is the current onboard estimate. Neither is an independently surveyed flight result. This website’s small recorded excerpt below is not the full desktop ground station.
